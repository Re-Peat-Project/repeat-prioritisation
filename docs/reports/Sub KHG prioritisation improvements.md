# Shrink the units, then sharpen the inputs

The sub-KHG ranking in `repeat-prioritisation/subkhg/` reads its data on a 30 m grid but collapses it to one value per unit of **101 to 1,358 km²**. The unit size comes from a cap of 30 units per KHG in `repeat-delineation`, not from any hydrological criterion. The input with the largest need weight (MODIS burned area, weight 2.0) is 500 m data, and the water-balance inputs are 4 to 25 km. Adding finer data alone will therefore change little. The gains come from three changes made together. First, redraw units as canal-bounded compartments nested inside sub-KHG, with a size floor derived from canal-block reach instead of a count cap. Second, compute indicators per km² of peat with a within-unit statistic. Third, swap the 500 m burn input for a 20 m Sentinel-2 burned-area product. Before any method work, two quick fixes bring the pipeline back in line with project rules. Peat depth (weight 2.0, floored at 50 cm, Spearman 0.67 with the need score in Kampar–Gaung) currently drives the ranking. Fire is counted over the whole unit instead of on peat. Neither existing site run has an outcome check, because `years.validation` is empty. Much of the hydrology code is duplicated between `repeat-delineation`, `repeat-pump`, `repeat-neraca` and `repeat-prioritisation`, and some of it belongs in `repeat-common`. All weights discussed here are provisional and have not been agreed with the team. The two site rankings (Kampar–Gaung, Sugihan–Lumpur) are test runs, not findings about where to intervene.

## A 30-unit cap sets unit size at 100 to 1,360 km²

`repeat-prioritisation` does not create sub-KHG units. The sub-KHG preset reads polygons from `repeat-delineation` (`units: {mode: vector}`, `grid_res_m: 30`, `repeat-prioritisation/subkhg/run.py:13-22`). The in-program `subkhg` mode was removed (`repeat-prioritisation/common/pipeline.py:189-192`). `repeat-delineation` burns rivers and canals 3 m into the DTM and fills pits and depressions. It then computes D8 flow with pysheds and traces each cell to its first outlet: a mapped river, an outlet-class canal or a DEM stream of order k or higher. Pieces under `min_area_km2` are merged into the neighbour with the longest shared divide. With `min_area_km2: auto`, the minimum grows by 10% steps from 5 km² until no more than `target_count[1]` = 30 units remain (`repeat-delineation/README.md:36-47`; defaults at `repeat-delineation/src/repeat_delineation/delineate.py:35-42`).

The two real runs show what that cap does. **Kampar–Gaung (KHG 71) went from 5,581 pieces to 26 units** with an automatic minimum of 127.7 km². Unit areas run from 132.5 to 884.7 km² (median 201.9), and the only outlets were 118 primary canal segments and 16 river segments. **Sugihan–Lumpur went from 6,219 pieces to 28 units** of 101.2 to 1,357.7 km² (both from `delineation_report.json` in `repeat-prioritisation/data_local/out_delineate*/`). In both runs the outlet-order loop stopped at its first attempt, "mapped channels" (`delineate.py:340-362`). The research team infers that DEM streams added no boundary, so the delineation is in practice "primary-canal and river catchments, merged down to 30". The Kampar runbook notes the weakness itself: units of 132–885 km² are large, the merge follows the longest shared divide and not a sub-basin hierarchy, and no official sub-KHG layout exists to compare against (`re-peat/sites/kampar_gaung/README.md:125`). Its minimum-area sweep (247 units at 5 km², 35 at 100 km², 9 at 300 km², `README.md:66-68`) shows that the unit count can be chosen freely. Nothing in the method fixes it.

Inside those units, the information resolution of most indicators is coarser than the 30 m grid. The table summarises the audit in `current_subkhg_pipeline.md` (computation in `repeat-prioritisation/common/indicators.py`, weights at `common/scoring.py:13-38` and `subkhg/prioritise.py:37-47`).

| Indicator | Source | Information resolution | Sub-KHG weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| `u_fire_freq` | MODIS MCD64A1 | 500 m, nearest-resampled to 30 m | 2.0 |
| `i_peat_depth` | BRGM peat-depth model (site file) | about 10 m, but floored at 50 cm | 2.0 |
| `u_ipg_gap` | Canals plus DTM slope | DTM about 12 m | 1.5 |
| `f_reach` (moved into need) | Canal vectors, 200 m reach | vector on 30 m grid | 1.5 |
| `u_ndmi` | Sentinel-2 L2A | 20 m default, **60 m in both site configs** | 1.0 |
| `u_hotspot` | FIRMS VIIRS S-NPP | points | 1.0 |
| `u_tma`, `u_tma_recession` | SiTMAT stations | points, 2026 only | 1.0 each |

The research team infers that the "full indicator set at 30 m" claim (`subkhg/run.py:2`) describes the cache grid, not the information. The site configs coarsen Sentinel-2 to 60 m "to keep the test cheap" (`re-peat/sites/kampar_gaung/subkhg.yaml`). The runbook justifies this by unit size: "the units are 130+ km2, so 60 m is enough" (`re-peat/sites/kampar_gaung/README.md:73`). Coarse units are being used to justify coarse inputs. Hydrology enters only as distances and geometry: canal density, canal reach, distance to water, canal slope and height above water. The flow accumulation and stream order that delineation already writes (`static/flow_accumulation.tif`, `static/stream_order.tif`) feed no indicator.

## Two indicators break project rules, and neither run has an outcome check

**Peat depth drives part of the ranking.** `i_peat_depth` has weight 2.0 (`repeat-prioritisation/common/scoring.py:29`). It has a Spearman correlation of 0.67 with the need score in Kampar–Gaung and 0.44 in Sugihan–Lumpur, and correlates at −0.87 with `f_lift` (`re-peat/sites/kampar_gaung/reports/kampar_gaung.md:69`; `sugihan_lumpur.md:69`). The Kampar raster floors at 50 cm: 37% of pixels, and 13 of the 25 units in the generated report, sit at the floor (`re-peat/sites/kampar_gaung/README.md:113-125`). The delineation produced 26 units; the report counts 25 in several statistics. AGENTS.md states that partial-coverage peat depth must not drive the ranking. Peat depth also covers only part of several of the 12 main KHG. Bounding-box coverage is 0.77 for Pungurbesar–Kapuas and 0.33 for Kahayan–Sebangau, with none for Kapuas–Barito or Buru Mappi–Buru Obaa (`sibling_repos_and_data.md`, Q6 table, read from rasterio bounds; bbox share overstates real coverage).

**Fire is not counted on peat.** `u_hotspot` divides by total unit area and `u_fire_freq` averages over all unit pixels (`repeat-prioritisation/common/indicators.py:118-141`). A grep for peat-extent or KLH hotspot inputs in `common/` and `subkhg/` returned nothing. The national profile follows the AGENTS.md convention: fire is counted on peat and divided by the GFW composite peat area, and the KLH 2026 hotspots are a separate indicator. `repeat_common.fetch_peat_extent` already provides the peat layer.

**No outcome check exists.** `validate` (AUC, top-20% capture and Spearman against held-out burned fraction and hotspots, `common/scoring.py:259-285`) returned `{}` in both `laporan.json` files because `years.validation: []`. Both runs used baseline 2019–2021. The SiTMAT indicator covers 2026-04-01 to 2026-10-15, so it describes a different period from the other indicators (site `subkhg.yaml`).

Three smaller faults affect the same ranking. `u_canal_density` and `f_reach` correlate at 0.88 to 0.89 and both sit in the need group, with weights 1.0 and 1.5 (`kampar_gaung.md:69`, `sugihan_lumpur.md:69`). That counts canals twice. `group_score` re-weights over whichever indicators a unit has (`common/scoring.py:105-116`). Units with and without a SiTMAT station (17 of 26 in Kampar) are therefore scored on different indicator sets. Sugihan–Lumpur drops `u_tma` entirely, because 12 of 28 units fall under the 13-unit minimum (`common/scoring.py:100`). Tertile classes are cut over all units in a run (`subkhg/prioritise.py:155-161`), contradicting the README's "within each KHG by default" (`subkhg/README.md:72`). This is harmless only while a run holds one KHG. The site configs also wire in a `neraca_unit.csv` that is the bucket-experiment table with `neraca_skill_ok = 0`, not the contract C5 table (`re-peat/docs/11-data-contracts.md` C5; `re-peat/docs/12-parked.md`). It gets no weight today, but it should not be on the input list.

Published work supports the indicators RE-PEAT already uses and suggests how to treat them. Drained peat in Sumatra and Kalimantan had up to 13 times the VIIRS hotspot density of undrained peat. The relation to canal density is curvilinear, and heavily drained plantations burn less than moderately drained land ([Salmayenti et al. 2025](https://consensus.app/papers/details/09d310be4c7d595dbeaf33815360f04b/?utm_source=claude_desktop), abstract only). Peat more than 300 m from canals had less water-table drawdown and burnt less often than peat 50–200 m away ([Sinclair et al. 2020](https://consensus.app/papers/details/26721908dcec5f0b9d2d9a6867958696/?utm_source=claude_desktop), abstract only). The research team infers that canal influence should be one saturating indicator (share of peat within a reach of canals, by canal class), replacing the current density-plus-reach pair. Where water-table data exist, an emission score is cheap to compute. An Indonesian meta-analysis found that a 10 cm deeper water table adds 2.7 Mg CO₂/ha/yr of total soil CO₂ ([Novita et al. 2021](https://consensus.app/papers/details/d587fd78312a590f9cd75b0984ffc158/?utm_source=claude_desktop), abstract only). Targeting the most drained land first gave several-fold larger early gains in Denmark ([Koch et al. 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/a08490a00376543d9a80ad0db15032a2/?utm_source=claude_desktop), abstract only) and in a global optimisation ([Ury et al. 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/24afaaa17eb25ae4bf1ef9bc4b319c99/?utm_source=claude_desktop), abstract only). Ury et al. also report that optimising for water quality or flood mitigation instead of GHG gave 62% and 44% less emission reduction. The research team infers that fire risk and emission avoidance should stay separate sub-scores so the trade-off is visible. SiTMAT supports an emission score only as a 2026, concession-biased current-state layer: 1,159 of 1,284 stations are concession stations (`repeat-sitmat/sitmat_out/processed/qc_report.txt`).

## Canal compartments nested inside sub-KHG fit flat peat domes

Regulation defines no "sub-KHG". PermenLHK P.14/2017 sets KHG boundaries from order-1 rivers, a 30 m DTM/SRTM and DAS boundaries. It splits each KHG into fungsi lindung and fungsi budidaya, with at least 30% lindung starting from one or more dome peaks ([P.14/2017](https://jdih.kehutanan.go.id/new2/uploads/files/P.14.pdf)). P.10/2019 determines the dome peak by a Darcy-based water balance. It requires topography with "interval kontur 0,5 meter (Data LiDAR atau World DEM...)" ([P.10/2019](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/344756/P.10-2019%20KHG.pdf)). The fetched text of P.14 does not mention sub-KHG or sub-DAS. The research team infers that these regulatory layers (lindung/budidaya, dome peak) are attributes a sub-KHG tool can carry, and that none of them is a hydrological compartment.

Terrain is the binding constraint on flat domes. Saxon et al. report adjusted 5 m DTM RMSE of 0.07–0.23 m for two Indonesian domes. The text and appendix tables swap which site has which value, so only the range is safe to quote. They also state that "each peat dome is a single, internally connected hydrological unit" ([Saxon et al., Mires and Peat](https://www.mires-and-peat.net/api/v1/articles/128831-3d-mapping-of-lowland-coastal-peat-domes-in-indonesia.pdf)). The Vernimmen et al. LiDAR-based DTM of eastern Sumatra has an estimated vertical accuracy of 0.25–0.61 m at 100 m ([Vernimmen et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7227361/)). The project's own corrected DEMNAS reaches 1.2 to 2.8 m RMSE against ICESat-2/GEDI, and no site meets the 0.25 m target, which is why `repeat-dem` is paused pending airborne LiDAR (`repeat-dem/AGENTS.md`, section on why the repo is paused). Dome relief is usually described as a few metres over kilometres, though that phrasing reached the researchers only as a search snippet ([ITPC](https://www.tropicalpeatlands.org/knowledge/publication/11463_35273/), unverified). The research team infers that DTM-derived divides on such surfaces can move by kilometres under small errors. Mapped canals and rivers should therefore set the boundaries, and the DTM should be used for dome and crest position.

The closest precedent is Cahyono, Aditya and Istarno (2022, UGM). They delineated **16 sub-PHU over 451,507 ha of Kahayan–Sebangau**, using streamlines, outlet channels, peat depth, slope and network connectivity on a 50 cm LiDAR DTM plus WorldDEM, then ranked them by ordinal scores ([Cahyono et al. 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-445x/11/7/1094)). That averages about 28,000 ha per unit, coarser than RE-PEAT's current units. It also used peat depth as a delineation input, which carries the same coverage risk for KHG without full depth data.

Canal-block reach gives a physical basis for unit size. Modelled block effect on the water table reached about 600 m from the nearest dam: 3 cm at 400 m, 1 mm at 1 km ([Urzainki et al. 2023](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/20/2099/2023/)). Field results vary widely. Sutikno et al. (2019) found a radius of about 170 m from dipwells ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331784577_The_effectiveness_of_canal_blocking_for_hydrological_restoration_in_tropical_peatland)). Sutikno et al. (2020), at Pulau Tebing Tinggi, found wetting about 1 m perpendicular to the canal, reaching 201 m only when canal water rose by more than 0.6 m ([Sutikno et al. 2020](https://consensus.app/papers/details/fc251b41d014519fbb21196abaf972d5/?utm_source=claude_desktop), abstract only). Blocking in Siak raised groundwater significantly within less than 100 m ([Safitri et al. 2024](https://consensus.app/papers/details/d2d921ee801c54dea177ac54047209c5/?utm_source=claude_desktop), abstract only). Dam spacing along canals was 320–975 m in Jaenicke et al. ([2010](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11027-010-9214-5)). From these numbers the research team derives, as arithmetic and not a published standard, a block footprint of about 11 to 117 ha. A claim that one block wets 15–35 ha falls inside that range, but it reached the team only as a snippet of uncertain attribution and is unverified. The team also derives a P.14 survey cell of about 100–300 ha, below which peat-depth attributes cannot resolve anything. Together these suggest a floor of roughly **100–300 ha** per compartment. That is two to three orders of magnitude below the current 10,000–135,000 ha units. The team further infers that dome interiors more than about 1 km from any canal are hardly touched by blocking. Those can stay as single protection units, with drained zones split into compartments.

From this the research team proposes a two-level scheme, which is their inference and not a tested method. Sub-KHG units stay as the regulatory and reporting level, still bounded by rivers and primary canals, as the 2021 field checks found (`repeat-delineation/src/repeat_delineation/delineate.py:16-19`). Compartments nest inside them, bounded by primary and secondary canals (`KODE_KANAL` 1 and 2) with a DTM crest line as an internal divide. Tertiary canals stay an attribute, since as boundaries they "would cut every block into its own unit" (`re-peat/sites/kampar_gaung/README.md:66-68`). Distance-to-canal bands (0–200, 200–600, 600–1,000, >1,000 m) express block reach inside each compartment without forcing the unit size to match it. The build plan already records this as the unapplied "single hierarchy" decision, KHG to sub-KHG to compartment to distance bands (`re-peat/docs/08-build-plan.md:91-95`). The research team proposes that the size floor replace `target_count`, since the cap is the root cause of the coarse units.

Depression handling should change at the same time. Delineation fills (`delineate.py:289`), and the build plan's switch to breaching has not been applied (`re-peat/docs/08-build-plan.md:100`). WhiteboxTools documents breaching as preferable to filling "in most cases" and offers FillBurn for canal vectors, Isobasins for near-equal basins and Subbasins by stream link ([WhiteboxTools API](https://jblindsay.github.io/wbt_nim/wbt_nim.html)). GRASS `r.watershed` uses a least-cost search that needs no sink filling and "will generate better basin results" where divides are flat ([GRASS manual](https://grass.osgeo.org/grass-stable/manuals/r.watershed.html)). `repeat-pump` already hydro-conditions with least-cost breaching in WhiteboxTools (`repeat-pump/repeat_pump/core/hydro.py`, docstring). The research team also infers that two DTM products are needed: a canal-free surface for dome shape and crest, as Saxon et al. edited canals out, and a canal-burned surface for routing. No peer-reviewed comparison of fill against breach, or of canal-burned against DTM-only delineation, on tropical peat was found.

Superpixels (SLIC, compact watershed in scikit-image) produce homogeneous patches on any raster stack ([scikit-image](https://scikit-image.org/docs/stable/auto_examples/segmentation/plot_segmentations.html)), but no source applied them to tropical peat management units. The research team infers that they ignore flow connectivity and should at most run inside compartments. Hexagons, already supported at 1 km² (`repeat-common/src/repeat_common/grid.py:66-126`), serve a different role: a second zoning to test rank stability. Tan et al. found that restoration benefits computed on 1 km² cells shrank but stayed of comparable magnitude when units moved to village or catchment boundaries ([Tan et al. 2022](https://consensus.app/papers/details/74739963afc85692869227b54025e4eb/?utm_source=claude_desktop), abstract only). No study testing zoning effects on tropical peat rankings was found.

## Few new datasets earn their place, led by 20 m burn scars

The most useful acquisition is the Nusantara Atlas monthly burned area. It is Sentinel-2 based, 20 m with a 6.25 ha minimum mapping unit, nationwide from January 2019 to December 2024 on Zenodo, with monthly updates on the platform. Its 2019 validation gave user's accuracy of 98.7% and producer's accuracy of 65.3%, so it misses about a third of burned area ([PLOS ONE 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0331831); [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/19104282)). The dataset licence is not stated. Burn frequency at 20 m would replace the 500 m MODIS input that carries the largest need weight, and would give the held-out validation a fire layer fine enough for compartments. The local `burnt_area_2015_2026_Indonesia.gpkg` (584,723 polygons) has no recorded source. Check whether it is already Nusantara before fetching anything.

For land cover and time since disturbance, JRC Tropical Moist Forest v1_2025 (30 m, 1990–2025, oil palm and rubber plantation classes, free use with citation; [JRC TMF](https://forobs.jrc.ec.europa.eu/TMF/data.php)) addresses the known flaw that `i_forest` counts plantations as forest (`re-peat/sites/kampar_gaung/README.md:118-119`). Hansen GFC v1.13 (30 m, loss year to 2025, CC BY 4.0; [GEE](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/UMD_hansen_global_forest_change_2025_v1_13)) and the Descals et al. oil palm planting year (30 m, planting-year RMSE 2.65 years, dataset licence not stated; [ESSD 2024](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/16/5111/2024/)) are alternatives. Dynamic World offers a near-real-time flooded-vegetation probability at 10 m, but with no published accuracy figure ([GEE](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/GOOGLE_DYNAMICWORLD_V1)). Restoring Sentinel-2 NDMI to its 20 m default costs nothing extra.

For terrain, the high-resolution layers are already on disk but outside the workspace. BRGM DTMs (10 m UTM in Kalimantan, about 12 m in Sumatra) and peat-depth models sit in the REPEAT-LH folder. Their bounding boxes cover 8 of the 11 resolved main KHG, Kahayan–Sebangau only by half, and Kapuas–Barito and Buru Mappi–Buru Obaa not at all (`sibling_repos_and_data.md`, Q6). The Pulau Padang LiDAR DTM (2 m, stated RMSE 0.35 m, datum unconfirmed) and the BRGM 0.4 m contours for 108 KHG (`_data/kontur_108khg_BRGM.gpkg`) are present but unused by sub-KHG configs. None of these appear in `re-peat/sites/data_catalog.csv`, and the source and vertical datum of the BRGM DTMs are not documented. For the KHG without a BRGM DTM, the `repeat-dem` comparison found no clear winner over Kahayan–Sebangau (about 2.1 to 2.2 m RMS). It recommends FABDEM or GLO-30 as the dome-scale base surface, mapped canals and rivers as unit boundaries, and LiDAR or survey for siting (`repeat-dem/AGENTS.md`, update 2026-10-15). FABDEM is CC BY-NC-SA ([GEE community catalog](https://gee-community-catalog.org/projects/fabdem/)). Copernicus GLO-30 2024_1 is a surface model that includes canopy ([GEE](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/COPERNICUS_DEM_GLO30_2024_1)). For eastern Sumatra, the openly deposited Vernimmen DTM (100 m, 2017 LiDAR; [Mendeley](https://doi.org/10.17632/72r3bvdd3r.1), licence not stated) can serve as a dome-scale check. Claims about DEMNAS accuracy (8.17 m against GPS) and about the Vernimmen 2019 method statistics reached the researchers only as search summaries and are unverified.

Several candidates do not fit the lean scope. No open Sentinel-1 water-table map for Indonesian peat at 100 m or finer was found. The Hooijer and Vernimmen 2026 water-table maps are 10 km and cover forested peat only ([Mendeley](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/69mbg22fxf/1)). BRGM's PRIMS and its groundwater devices were seen only in search summaries, with no download found (unverified). InSAR subsidence at 90 m (Hoyt et al. 2020) has no data link, and the resolution figure itself comes from a search summary. The Dadap et al. canal map (5 m, 2017) is CC BY-ND, so it suits a completeness check of the 2025 OSM-derived canal layer, not redistribution ([NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10556931)). Global peat-depth models are 1 km or coarser ([Biogeosciences 2026](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/23/2959/2026/)), and depth must not drive the ranking anyway. Process models with block optimisation (Urzainki et al. 2020 found 10 optimised blocks matched 60 random ones; [Biogeosciences preprint](https://luk.staff.ugm.ac.id/rawa/PLG/bg-2020-83.pdf)) are heavy and unvalidated against field data. The lesson to carry over is the buffer rule, not the model.

## Flow routing, IPG and data readers are duplicated across repos

The chain DTM → delineation → neraca → prioritisation → pump ran end to end on Kampar–Gaung. Several links are weak or duplicated, and the duplicates disagree. Flow routing exists twice: pysheds with fill in delineation (`delineate.py:289`) and WhiteboxTools with breach in pump (`repeat-pump/repeat_pump/core/hydro.py`). The two give different flow on the same DTM. IPG block spacing exists twice with different caps. Prioritisation uses `clip(dA / slope, 50, 2000)` (`repeat-prioritisation/common/indicators.py:255`). Pump proposes `max_spacing_m: 500` (`repeat-pump/configs/proposed_defaults.yaml:24`). Kampar has 11 of 25 units at the 2,000 m cap (`kampar_gaung.md:102-103`). Sentinel-2 wetness is read twice: `repeat_common.sources.ndmi_cloud` (Planetary Computer, 20 m) and pump's `cloudfetch.py` (Earth Search, 10 m, NDWI plus NDMI). MODIS burned area is also read twice (`sources.burned_cloud`, pump `burnfetch.py`). The Boussinesq solver exists in pump and in a neraca testbed copy, and `auto_utm` has three extra copies (`repeat-dem/demnas_dtm.py:205`, the dem QGIS plugin `core/grid.py:79`, `repeat-neraca/src/repeat_neraca/inputs.py:67`). Three field names describe the same canal class: `KET_KANAL` in the delineation and neraca configs, `KODE_KANAL` in AGENTS.md, and `canal_class` in pump. They map one to one (KODE 1 = Kanal Primer, 43,217 lines; 2 = Sekunder; 3 = Tersier), counted from `_data/kanal_osgm_khg_existing_Indonesia.gpkg`. Pump, dem and sitmat import nothing from `repeat_common`.

`repeat-common`'s own rule is to move code there once two modules use it (`repeat-common/README.md`). Under that rule, hydro-conditioning and flow accumulation, the IPG formula, the Sentinel-2 and MODIS readers, and a canal-class normaliser all qualify. The research team adds that this has a methodological payoff: compartments and pump block siting would rest on the same routed surface, and IPG would mean the same thing in the ranking and in the block design. Two parked pieces matter for higher resolution. The `dtm_smooth.json` sidecar (datum, RMSE) is not written, and no site config reads `repeat-dem` output (`re-peat/docs/12-parked.md`; `re-peat/docs/11-data-contracts.md` C2). `repeat_common.contracts` is also parked. Without either, a sub-KHG unit cannot record whether its slope and IPG values rest on LiDAR, a BRGM DTM or FABDEM.

The division of labour between repos also clarifies what sub-KHG prioritisation should not attempt. Pump stage 6 already scores canal segments inside selected sub-KHG, using a moisture ring buffer, upstream flow accumulation, AHP or direct weights and sensitivity (`repeat-pump/repeat_pump/core/prioritise.py`, docstring). It reads `subkhg_priority.csv` and gives every unit equal priority when that file is missing (`repeat-pump/README.md`). Both site configs have `priority_export` commented out, and no such file exists in the outputs. The research team infers that the sub-KHG module's job is to rank compartments and pass them on, leaving segment and block siting to pump. Neraca contributes one credible number per KHG, the dry-season deficit. Its per-unit output has no spatial skill (held-out RMSE 16.5 cm against a 15.3 cm no-skill baseline in Kampar; `kampar_gaung.md:14-21`), so per-sub-KHG deficit stays parked. `repeat-vis` has no data link to sub-KHG work.

Housekeeping blocks reproducibility, which AGENTS.md requires. `REPEAT_DATA` means the workspace `_data` in AGENTS.md but the REPEAT-LH folder in the Kampar runbook. `REPEAT_WORK` means `re-peat/reports` in AGENTS.md, while the outputs sit in `repeat-prioritisation/data_local/`. The runbook and the generated Kampar report disagree on several numbers: median `f_water_dist` 13 km against 8,496 m, blocks needed 1,759 against 1,642, `f_reach`–density correlation 0.94 against 0.884. They were probably produced by different runs. Sugihan–Lumpur was not re-run from the hub configs (`re-peat/docs/12-parked.md:34`). The indicator computation has no unit test (grep of `repeat-prioritisation/tests/`), so a change of grid or aggregation cannot be regression-checked.

## Conclusion

The resolution problem is mainly a unit problem. The pipeline already holds 10–30 m layers (WorldCover, JRC, canal vectors, BRGM DTM) and averages them over areas the size of a small regency. The cap that sets that size is a single parameter. The physical evidence on block reach (hundreds of metres, negligible beyond about 1 km) points to compartments in the 100–300 ha range for drained land. That range is the research team's derivation, not a published standard. Even so, it gives the delineation a defensible criterion where it now has none. Finer units only pay off with fine inputs. The 20 m burned-area series is the single acquisition that changes both the ranking and the ability to validate it.

The order of work matters. Removing peat depth from the need score and masking fire to peat will likely reorder the existing test rankings. If that happens before any method change, the team can see how much the current top units owed to a floored depth raster. A held-out fire check on Nusantara burns, reported separately for ENSO-neutral and El Niño years (the canal–fire relation weakens in El Niño years per Salmayenti et al.), then gives the first evidence for any weight set. Every weight remains provisional until then.
